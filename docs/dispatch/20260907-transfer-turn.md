## 5. The exact ladder — heavy compute

```text
Your ticket is #639. Read the issue in full.

Get axis L=5 and diamond L=5 by brute force (2^25). That is the immediate win
and it triples the strength of the acceptance gate that #636 will be judged on.

The trap, and it is the reason this ticket is worded the way it is: the obvious
way to reach L=6 is a column-by-column DP carrying connectivity state -- which
IS the connectivity transfer matrix, the exact thing this ladder exists to
validate. Producing L=6 that way and then using it to check #636 is circular and
would silently destroy the gate. Do not do it.

The honest route to L=6 is symmetry reduction: the LxL torus has a
dihedral-times-translation automorphism group of order 8L^2 and D(C) is
invariant under it, so orbits can be counted once with multiplicity. At L=6 that
takes 6.9e10 down to about 2.4e8, which is reachable. If you use it, verify it
against the committed L=3 integers BEFORE trusting a new rung.

If neither works, "L=6 is out of independent reach, here is why" is the correct
deliverable. A circular L=6 is worse than none.

C++ is acceptable here; scripts/exact_c4_self_matching_polynomial.cpp shows the
precedent. Conventions and the definition of D(C) must match
scripts/exact_matching_polynomial.py exactly.
```
