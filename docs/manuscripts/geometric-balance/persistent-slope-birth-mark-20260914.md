# The rank-one slope is a persistent birth mark

2026-09-14.  Exact finite statement for the uniform-label birth process.  It connects the projective homology observable to the two sharp birth times without introducing a new sampling protocol.

## 1. The slope cannot change while rank remains one

Let `S_p={v:U_v<=p}` under one realization of continuous iid labels.  Its ambient NN homology image

\[
A(p)=\operatorname{im}[H_1(G_{S_p})\to H_1(T^2)]
\]

is monotone by inclusion as `p` increases.

Let `T_1<=T_2` be the first times rank is at least one and two.  On the event `T_1<T_2`, for every

\[
T_1\le p<T_2
\]

the image `A(p)` is one-dimensional.  Since

\[
A(p)\subseteq A(q),\qquad p<q,
\]

and both spaces have dimension one, they are equal.  Therefore there is a unique random projective line `L` such that

\[
\boxed{A(p)=L\quad\text{for all }T_1\le p<T_2.}                \tag{1.1}
\]

The rank-one homology direction is selected at the first birth and remains frozen until the second birth.  It is not a direction that can wander continuously inside the plateau.

If `T_1=T_2`, the rank-one interval has zero length.  We may assign `L` an arbitrary cemetery value there; every identity below multiplies its mark by `T_2-T_1` and is unaffected.

## 2. Persistent 4/8 reflection including the slope mark

For reflected labels `V=1-U`, the existing persistent digital-Alexander theorem gives

\[
T_1^8(V)=1-T_2^4(U),
\qquad
T_2^8(V)=1-T_1^4(U).                                          \tag{2.1}
\]

At every complementary rank-one configuration, the black NN and white matching homology lines are equal.  Since that line is constant throughout the plateau on either process,

\[
\boxed{
(T_1^8(V),T_2^8(V),L_8(V))
=(1-T_2^4(U),1-T_1^4(U),L_4(U))}                              \tag{2.2}
\]

on nonzero-length rank-one plateaux.

Thus the plateau duration and slope mark are complement-even, while the plateau centre is complement-odd.

## 3. Fixed-p slope law is a slice through one marked interval

For any projective test function `f(L)`, define

\[
A_f(p)=E\left[1_{\{r(p)=1\}}f(L)\right].                       \tag{3.1}
\]

Using the persistent mark,

\[
\boxed{
A_f(p)=E\left[1_{\{T_1\le p<T_2\}}f(L)\right].}               \tag{3.2}
\]

In particular the spin-four topological harmonic is obtained with

\[
f(L)=Z_4(L)=e^{i4\theta_L}.                                   \tag{3.3}
\]

This is useful conceptually: the `p`-dependent rank-one slope distribution is not generated by slope transitions inside the plateau.  It is a length-biased slice of the joint birth-mark law `(T_1,T_2,L)`.

## 4. Exact integrated marked-gap identity

Integrate (3.2) over `p` and apply Fubini:

\[
\boxed{
\int_0^1 A_f(p)\,dp
=E\left[(T_2-T_1)f(L)\right].}                                \tag{4.1}
\]

For `f=1` this is the already-used rank-one area identity

\[
\int_0^1P_1(p)\,dp=E(T_2-T_1).                                \tag{4.2}
\]

For the slope harmonic,

\[
\boxed{
\int_0^1 A_4(p)\,dp
=E\left[(T_2-T_1)e^{i4\theta_L}\right].}                       \tag{4.3}
\]

Therefore the ratio

\[
\boxed{
\overline H_4^{gap}
:=\frac{\int_0^1A_4(p)\,dp}{\int_0^1P_1(p)\,dp}
=\frac{E[(T_2-T_1)e^{i4\theta_L}]}{E(T_2-T_1)}}               \tag{4.4}
\]

is the spin-four harmonic of the slope when rank-one plateaux are sampled proportionally to their `p`-length.

This is a third sampling protocol, distinct from:

- a fixed-`p` conditional rank-one slope;
- the first-birth slope mark without duration weighting.

They need not have the same finite-size distribution.

## 5. Exact configuration-census formula

On an `N`-site finite torus, a fixed occupied set `S` of size `k` has Bernoulli weight

\[
p^k(1-p)^{N-k}.
\]

Its integral over `p` is

\[
\int_0^1p^k(1-p)^{N-k}dp
=\frac{k!(N-k)!}{(N+1)!}
=\frac1{(N+1)\binom Nk}.                                      \tag{5.1}
\]

Hence the integrated marked plateau can be read from a deterministic configuration census with no permutation enumeration:

\[
\boxed{
\int_0^1 A_f(p)dp
=\sum_{S:r(S)=1}
\frac{f(L(S))}{(N+1)\binom N{|S|}}.}                          \tag{5.2}
\]

The unmarked version gives `E(T_2-T_1)` exactly.

## 7. Direction selection in elongated tori

The projective line `L` is the natural mark of the first topological birth.  In a geometry where the homological free-energy race has one unique cheapest primitive direction, the conjectural/derived directional theory predicts concentration of `L` on that line.

If two primitive directions have comparable rare-event fugacity, the first birth chooses between them, but once one line is chosen the process cannot switch to the other while remaining rank one.  The appearance of a nonparallel class is precisely the transition to rank two.

This is the exact dynamic underpinning of the projective hard-core-gas picture: the first birth selects the projective sector, and the second birth ends it.
