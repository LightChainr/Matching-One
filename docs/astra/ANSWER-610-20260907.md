## Q1: a scalar consistency identity comes before choosing a functional family

Let `v01=(Q1-Q0)/h0`, `v12=(Q2-Q1)/h1`, and `C=2/(h0+h1)`.
For **any one fixed linear functional** ell,

```text
ell(2 Q[s0,s1,s2]) = C [ell(v12) - ell(v01)].
```

If two first differences differ from their model values `m0,m1` by relative
errors at most `e`, the relative second-difference error is bounded by

```text
e (|m0|+|m1|)/|m1-m0|.
```

For the published model and these two intervals this bound is **10.4% at
e=3.8%**. It rules out a 44–58% discrepancy for the *same scalar functional*,
regardless of whether the generating function is a sum of powers, a logarithmic
correction, or a stretched exponential. There is no two-power parameter search
to perform on that literal premise.

Directly differencing the published first amplitudes gives:

| lineage | difference of first amplitudes | exponential prediction | ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 65,130,325 | 0.00147254190 | 0.00146051349 | 1.00823574 |
| 85,170,425 | 0.00115337029 | 0.00112583403 | 1.02445854 |

### Where the comparison changes object

`p582_amplitude_law.amplitude_points` fits each first difference against
`[1,Q_base,g]`. `second_difference_check` fits curvature against `[1,Q_middle,g]`,
with a different covariance metric too. Thus `g` being fixed does **not** make
the extracted amplitude a fixed covector applied to `Q`.

The change can be quantified without a new model. Write the fitted first step as

```text
v01 = alpha0 1 + beta0 Q0 + a0 g + r0,
k = 1 + h0 beta0,
Q1 = k Q0 + h0 alpha0 1 + h0 a0 g + h0 r0.
```

When `k != 0`, in the middle chart the **same** first step is

```text
v01 = (alpha0/k)1 + (beta0/k)Q1 + (a0/k)g + r0/k.
```

If ell_C is the curvature estimator, satisfying `ell_C(1)=ell_C(Q1)=0` and
`ell_C(g)=1`, then, exactly,

```text
a_curvature = C [a1 - a0/k] + C [ell_C(r1) - ell_C(r0)/k].
```

The omitted term in the original comparison is primarily the already measured
width contraction `k≈0.78`. No new exponent is required to derive it.

## Q2: exact pair symmetry, but no protected single-model shape split

Define the reversed *ordered matching pair* by exchanging NN and NN+NNN roles.
For the same wrapping convention,

```text
M_hat(p) = -M_G(1-p),
F_hat(p) = 1-F_G(1-p),
Q_hat(u) = 1-Q_G(1-u).
```

These follow by complementing configurations and exchanging the two graphs.
They relate two models. They do not imply `Q_G(u)+Q_G(1-u)=1`.
Exact enumeration of the honest 3x3 square-site torus gives
`M_G(1/2)=-21/64`, `F_G(1/2)=43/128`, directly refuting that same-model symmetry.
The involution obtained by defining `p -> Q_G(1-F_G(p))` exists for **any**
strictly increasing CDF; it cannot independently explain this dataset.

On the doubled model space one can define parity sectors. That construction
alone gives neither an invariant single-model measure nor an identification
of the empirically fitted `g` with one sector. Apparent parity of `g` is also
not invariant under adding its fitted affine nuisance directions. In particular,
removing `span{1,Q_base}` does not remove the whole median-symmetric subspace.

### Two identities that reduce the empirical square-bond mystery

On the self-dual square-bond torus at p=1/2, put `X=r-1`, `O=wrap_either`.
Configuration-wise `r∈{0,1,2}` and

```text
O = 1 + (X-X^2)/2.
```

Geometric duality sends `X -> -X` and `B_even -> B_even`. Therefore

```text
Cov(O,X)      = Var(X)/2 = P(r=2),
Cov(O,B_even) = -Cov(X^2,B_even)/2.
```

The nearly constant first covariance is the already present cross-wrapping
probability. It is not an independent coupling measurement between two
unrelated observables. L=2 rational enumeration gives `69/256` for all three
quantities in the first identity and `-427/32768` for both sides of the second.

Furthermore, for **any deterministic nested coordinate-reveal filtration of a
product Bernoulli measure**, two increasing functions have nonnegative
cross-increments `E[D_j f D_j g]`. Condition on previously revealed coordinates;
the conditional expectations are increasing functions of the newly revealed
independent coordinates. Their conditional covariance is nonnegative by Harris
association, and averaging proves the claim. Both `wrap_either` and `r-1` are
increasing. Thus the nonnegative ambient scale increments are forced in this
setting, at every p and size, without duality or a defect mechanism.

The small Betti covariance has no comparable monotonicity constraint. Signs of
its increments do not identify parity. A reveal filtration need not commute
with dual transport either. Finally, vanishing of *every* degree-one Betti
coefficient uses **edge transitivity plus duality**: transitivity makes them
equal, while duality sends a coefficient to the negative of its permuted mate.
Parity alone on an arbitrary nontransitive graph is insufficient.

The Harris input is standard; see Duminil-Copin's
[Bernoulli-percolation lectures, §2.1](https://www.ihes.fr/~duminil/publi/Courspeccot2.pdf).
The covariance identities and conditional-filtration argument above are the
applications used here, not a claim to a new correlation inequality.

## Q3: the lemma, including transfer matrices

Let R be a linear involution, `R G0=G0 R`, `R H R=-H`.
Require right and left endpoint invariance `R rho=rho`, `f^T R=f^T`.
No reversibility, positivity, stationary initial measure or inner-product
orthogonality is required for this algebraic statement. Then for all a,b,

```text
f^T exp(a G0) H exp(b G0) rho = 0.
```

Insert R on both sides; the expression equals its negative. This proves
pointwise Duhamel vanishing. More generally, the endpoint parities must be
equal for one odd insertion to vanish. An invariant readout alone is insufficient.

For the pure linear family `G(e)=G0+e H`, the whole invariant-endpoint response
is even in e, so every odd Taylor coefficient vanishes. The leading nonzero
order can be 2, 4, higher, or absent. There is **no sign theorem** for order 2.
`astra610_symmetry_check.py` supplies a three-state row-stochastic transfer
matrix with invariant center and complementary readouts and exact quadratic
coefficients `-9/128` and `+9/128` after four steps.

For `T(e)=T0+e V+...`, `[R,T0]=0` and `R V R=-V` imply

```text
d/de [f^T T(e)^m rho] at 0
 = sum_j f^T T0^(m-1-j) V T0^j rho = 0.
```

An affine generator is unnecessary. For normalized transfer expectations the
numerator and denominator must both have the required parity and the denominator
must be nonzero; varying boundaries or observables contribute their own derivatives.
All-orders evenness requires the full family relation `R T(e) R=T(-e)`, not
merely an odd first derivative.

**Uniform p is generally reflection-even:** if a spatial reflection commutes
with `T(p)` for all p, it also commutes with `dT/dp`. Thus this selection rule
does not suppress the ordinary thermal derivative. An explicitly reflected
antisymmetric site-probability perturbation is an eligible different experiment.
The square-site/matching pair exchange needs a separately established action on
the enlarged transfer state, not its name imported from P398.

## Q4: a useful bridge to p_c, without a correction-to-scaling ansatz

Here is a **conditional proof**, suitable for checking against #276, not a new
claim of a solved critical probability.

Assume a sequence of honest periodic square-cell tori with N vertices and
shortest nonzero period length ell_N, with `ell_N/log N -> infinity`.
Primitive Gaussian square tori have `ell_N=sqrt(N)`. Assume:

1. configuration-wise `r_G+r_hat=2` for the declared digital matching pair;
2. subcritical site-percolation connections on each infinite graph decay
   exponentially in distance at every fixed p below its own critical point;
3. `p_c(G)+p_c(hat G)=1`.

Any nonzero ambient cycle has a lift escaping a ball of radius, for example,
`ell_N/4-O(1)`. Until that exit the ball is embedded and its occupation law is
the ordinary infinite-graph product law. A union bound over N starting vertices
and assumption 2 gives, for fixed p below p_c(G),

```text
P_p(r_G>0) <= C(p) N exp[-c(p) ell_N] -> 0.
```

For fixed p above p_c(G), apply the same bound to the matching graph at 1-p.
Assumptions 1 and 3 yield `P_p(r_G<2) -> 0`. Since
`F_N(p)=E_p[r_G]/2`, it follows that `F_N(p)->0` below p_c and `->1` above it.

The finite rank is increasing and nonconstant in the occupied-site set. Along
some empty-to-full configuration chain a single-site increment is positive;
that pivotal configuration has positive Bernoulli weight for every `0<p<1`.
The product-measure derivative identity therefore gives `F_N'(p)>0`.
Consequently, for every fixed `0<u<1`,

```text
Q_N(u) -> p_c(G).
```

The argument also gives uniform convergence on each compact interior u-interval,
and convergence of the threshold random variable to a point mass. Any bounded
orientation-weight combination with weights summing to one has the same limit;
unbounded extrapolating weights require an additional bound.

The inputs are standard percolation facts, not consequences of the eight
productions. For source checking: [Duminil-Copin–Tassion, §1.2 “Site
percolation”](https://www.ihes.fr/~duminil/publi/sharpness%20perco%20Ising.pdf)
discuss the site adaptation of sharpness. [Grimmett–Li, equations (1.1)–(1.3)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/rsa.21226)
give the matching critical-point relation and its amenable context.
[Mertens–Ziff](https://arxiv.org/html/1603.07289) give the finite matching identity;
their introduction's analyticity motivation should not replace the probability
theorem needed for assumption 3.

This establishes a route from the finite law to the **location of its limit**,
not a numerical value, an L^-4 rate, an operator identity, or a closed form.
The shape pilot and the P398 quotient do not supply those missing statements.
If a certified numerical interval is the objective, #112's finite-event
criterion is the distinct route. If a physical explanation is the objective,
future experiments must compare a source with a readout that is not already
algebraically determined by that source, and must predict its transmission.
