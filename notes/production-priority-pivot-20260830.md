## E_top is now a plane question, not a missing-ray question

Branch `analysis/etop-c-clock-mixture-20260830` at scientific commit `36bf1fe` reuses production data plus the independent P205 prism. The one-coordinate A ray fails (`chi2=56.4497/10`, `p=1.69e-8`), while `A+C` survives (`10.3059/9`, `p=.326`). Adding lifetime `W` after `C` changes only `Delta chi2=.661` (`p=.416`). Thus the most useful new energy observation is one that tests whether an independently marked local singlet replaces `C` and fixes the plane coefficients.

The microscopic definition should be theory-led. Camia and Feng define a lattice percolation energy through a centered nearest-neighbour cluster-spin product and identify it through mixed correlations, not a directional one-point mean. This makes same-stream mixed moments more informative than simply enlarging the stopped H4 pilot: [The percolation energy field and its logarithmic partner](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16047).

## Default team allocation

For the next work block, a useful attention split is roughly **60% production acquisition/scoring, 25% existing-data mechanism synthesis and 15% exact/tool work**. This is a coordination default, not a quota. Exact or software work should jump the queue whenever it is the shortest route to one of the declared observations above; otherwise it remains parallel infrastructure rather than the headline result.

Every work-block summary should answer three short questions:

1. What new production observation or existing-data decision was obtained?
2. Which live model class moved, survived or was replaced?
3. What is the next observation that can distinguish the remaining mechanisms?

Passing more tests, adding another fixture or polishing a compiler is useful maintenance, but does not by itself answer any of these questions.
