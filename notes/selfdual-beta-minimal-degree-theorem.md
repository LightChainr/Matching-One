# Self-dual reliability polynomials: why the N=10 Beta law is forced at minimal degree

**Status:** exact polynomial theorem plus post-target application to PR #152.

The N=10 `Beta(3,3)` identity and the N=26 failure of `Beta(5,5)` admit a sharper algebraic interpretation than “one small quotient works and the next does not.”

The key variable is

\[
u=p(1-p).
\]

## 1. Every complement-odd polynomial factors through u

Let `M(p)` be a polynomial satisfying

\[
M(1-p)=-M(p).
\]

Then `p=1/2` is a root and

\[
Q(p)=\frac{M(p)}{2p-1}
\]

is invariant under `p -> 1-p`.

The invariant polynomial ring of this involution is generated by `u=p(1-p)`, so there is a unique polynomial `H` such that

\[
\boxed{M(p)=(2p-1)H(p(1-p)).}
\]

For a centered self-matching reliability function

\[
F(p)=\frac{1+M(p)}2,
\]

we have `M(0)=-1`, hence `H(0)=1`.

## 2. Minimum support forces a universal central-binomial prefix

Assume the event cannot occur with fewer than `s` occupied sites. Equivalently,

\[
F(p)=a_s p^s+O(p^{s+1})
\]

near `p=0`, where `a_s` is the number of successful configurations of minimum size `s`.

On the branch near `p=0`,

\[
2p-1=-\sqrt{1-4u}.
\]

Therefore

\[
H(u)=\frac{M(p)}{2p-1}
=\frac{1-2a_s p^s+O(p^{s+1})}{\sqrt{1-4u}}.
\]

Since `p=u+O(u^2)`, this gives

\[
H(u)
=(1-4u)^{-1/2}+O(u^s).
\]

Using the classical generating function

\[
(1-4u)^{-1/2}
=\sum_{j\ge0}{2j\choose j}u^j,
\]

we obtain the exact theorem

\[
\boxed{
H(u)=\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}{2j\choose j}u^j+O(u^s).
}
\]

Thus the first `s` coefficients of the symmetric quotient are not model dependent at all; they are forced by self-duality and the minimum successful occupation.

## 3. The first free coefficient counts minimum paths

Keeping the first non-universal term gives

\[
H(u)=\sum_{j=0}^{s}{2j\choose j}u^j-2a_s u^s+O(u^{s+1}).
\]

If `c_s=[u^s]H`, then

\[
\boxed{
a_s=\frac{{2s\choose s}-c_s}{2}.}
\]

This is the symmetric-quotient form of the minimum-layer reliability-signature count.

For an exact `Beta(s,s)` law, `H` stops at degree `s-1`, so `c_s=0` and

\[
a_s=\frac12{2s\choose s}={2s-1\choose s},
\]

recovering the majority-core minimum-path count in the reliability-signature note.

## 4. Minimal-degree theorem: Beta(s,s) is forced

Suppose in addition that

\[
\deg M\le 2s-1.
\]

Since `M=(2p-1)H(u)`, this means

\[
\deg_u H\le s-1.
\]

But every coefficient of `H` through degree `s-1` has already been fixed by the minimum-support theorem. Hence

\[
H(u)=\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}{2j\choose j}u^j
\]

with no remaining freedom.

This polynomial is exactly the centered `Beta(s,s)` / majority reliability law. Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
M(1-p)=-M(p),\\
F(p)=O(p^s),\\
\deg M\le2s-1
\end{array}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
F(p)=I_p(s,s).
}
\]

So `Beta(s,s)` is the **unique minimal-degree self-dual reliability polynomial with minimum support s**.

This is a more informative explanation than treating Beta as an independent functional ansatz.

## 5. N=10 is exactly the minimal-degree case

For the exact N=10 C4 self-matching quotient,

```text
s = 3
deg M = 5 = 2s-1.
```

The theorem therefore forces

\[
H_{10}(u)=1+2u+6u^2
\]

and hence `F=Beta(3,3)`.

The exact Beta identity is real and useful, but it should not by itself be interpreted as evidence for an infinite Beta family. Given self-duality, minimum support 3, and the observed degree collapse to 5, the Beta law has no remaining algebraic freedom.

The deeper N=10 question becomes:

> Why does this quotient's self-matching polynomial collapse all the way to the minimal allowed degree `2s-1`?

That is a graph/topology question, not a curve-fitting question.

## 6. N=26 exposes the new degrees of freedom explicitly

PR #152 gives the exact N=26 polynomial with

```text
s = 5
deg M = 19 > 2s-1 = 9.
```

Dividing by `2p-1` and rewriting in `u=p(1-p)` gives exactly

\[
\boxed{
H_{26}(u)
=1+2u+6u^2+20u^3+70u^4
+96u^5+170u^6+260u^7+260u^8+78u^9.
}
\]

The universal prefix is visible directly:

```text
1, 2, 6, 20, 70
= C(0,0), C(2,1), C(4,2), C(6,3), C(8,4).
```

The first model-dependent coefficient is `c_5=96`. The minimum-path formula gives

\[
a_5=\frac{{10\choose5}-96}{2}
=\frac{252-96}{2}
=78,
\]

exactly the count reported by PR #152.

Thus the N=26 Beta(5,5) failure is not arbitrary. The exact polynomial is

\[
M_{26}(p)
=M_{\mathrm{Beta}(5,5)}(p)
+(2p-1)u^5
\left(96+170u+260u^2+260u^3+78u^4\right).
\]

Because `u^5=p^5(1-p)^5`, this identity also explains the discrepancy factor observed in PR #152:

\[
p^5(1-p)^5(2p-1).
\]

No mysterious factorization remains: it is the universal form of the first allowed deformation away from the minimal-degree self-dual Beta law.

## 7. Positive quotient coefficients imply the unique physical center root

For N=26 all coefficients of `H_26(u)` are positive. Since

\[
0\le u=p(1-p)\le1/4
\]

for `p in [0,1]`,

\[
H_{26}(u)>0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\operatorname{sign}M_{26}(p)=\operatorname{sign}(2p-1)
\]

throughout the physical interval and the exact finite matching equation has the unique physical root

\[
p=1/2.
\]

This positivity is an additional exact structural property of N=26. It is not implied by self-duality alone and should be tested on the next exact self-matching quotients if feasible.

## 8. New research questions

The Beta-family question should be replaced by a hierarchy of sharper algebraic questions.

### A. Degree excess

Define

\[
\delta=\deg M-(2s-1).
\]

N=10 has `delta=0`; N=26 has `delta=10`.

What graph/topological property controls this degree excess?

### B. Symmetric quotient coefficients

For self-matching quotients, record the exact `H(u)` coefficient vector, not only `M(p)`.

Questions:

- Are the post-prefix coefficients always nonnegative?
- Is `H(u)>0` on `[0,1/4]` always true even if coefficients change sign?
- Can the coefficients be interpreted as counts of topological obstruction classes?
- Does the surface-topological polynomial program (#144) produce `H` naturally?

### C. First-deformation universality

For any minimum support `s`, the first possible deviation from the Beta law is forced to have

\[
M-M_{\rm Beta}
=(2p-1)p^s(1-p)^s R(p(1-p)).
\]

This should be used as the natural exact deformation basis before proposing generalized beta functions.

### D. Relation to activation signatures

The first free coefficient is exactly the minimum successful layer count. Higher `u` coefficients should be related to higher finite differences / interaction structure of the activation signature. Deriving that transform may connect this theorem to the Boolean Fourier/Banzhaf tangent program in PR #162.
