## 2. Step 4: the primary forecast — **supports**, with two caveats that matter

Estimator as pre-registered: basis `[1, Q_290, g_frozen]`, observation
`(Q_725 − Q_290)/log 2.5`, covariance `(S_290 + S_725)/log(2.5)²`, reported
number = the **`g` coefficient**.

```text
a_hat(290->725)  = -4.8083463e-04      se = 2.2941e-06
a0 (declared)    = -4.6808425e-04
a_hat - a0       = -1.2750e-05   =  -5.56 se   =  -2.72 %
3 se interval    = [-4.877168e-04, -4.739525e-04]
tolerance band   = [-4.914885e-04, -4.446800e-04]        (a0 ± 5 %)
OUTCOME          = supports_this_finite_forecast
```

The three-standard-error interval is **wholly inside** the band, so by #612's
declared rule the finite forecast is supported.

The frozen five-amplitude fit reproduces #609's pre-registered amplitude:
predicted `-4.68083771e-04` against the published `-4.68084249e-04`, agreeing to
seven significant figures (4.8e-10 apart — #609 rounded).

### Caveat 1 — "supports" is a 5 % statement, not a consistency statement

The measurement is **5.56 nominal standard errors** from the point forecast.
The band is ±2.34e-05 wide, i.e. ±10.2 se, so a 5.6 se miss still sits inside
it. Read literally: the one-exponent law predicts this amplitude to 2.7 %,
which passes a 5 % tolerance check and fails a nominal-error check. Either the
jackknife error understates the real uncertainty about tenfold, or the law
carries a genuine ~2.7 % bias at this step. #612 already warns this is "a
declared tolerance check with a nominal jackknife error, not a theorem about an
exponent or an exact-coverage test" — both readings are reported because they
lead to different next steps.

### Caveat 2 — the verdict is weighting-conditional

| | spin0 (primary) | equal (declared sensitivity) |
|---|---:|---:|
| `a_hat` | −4.80835e-04 | −4.24263e-04 |
| `a_hat − a0` | −5.56 se | +21.59 se |
| 3 se interval vs band | wholly inside | wholly **outside** |
| outcome | supports | stops |

The spin0 → equal shift is **5.66e-05**, i.e. **12 % of the amplitude and about
25 standard errors** — more than twice the width of the ±5 % band itself. So
the "supports" verdict holds under the weighting #582 declared primary and does
not survive the naive one. Two honest qualifications on the equal row: the band
was itself constructed under spin0, and the frozen fit's own equal-weighting
prediction is `-4.01403e-04`, not `-4.68084e-04`. The equal row is therefore a
measurement of the orientation-weighting **systematic**, which is the largest
error term in this comparison by a wide margin.

## 3. Step 5: the curvature — the 55 % "second scale" is gone, a 4 % misfit is not

`[1.79279, −3.14899, 1.35620]` are reproduced exactly.

* `P′` differs from the 1.547 target by **−0.672 %** — #612's "about 0.7 %",

  evidence that does.
* But the **measured** curvature is `1.0743e-03`: **+4.25 %** above `P′`
  (7.3 se of the curvature) and **+3.55 %** above the 1.547 target. Chart

* The residual-transport terms are negligible, as the identity predicts:

* The curvature's own residual after removing `[1, Q_290, g]` is `42.8` on 6 df
  — structured. The new 290→725 first difference's residual is `1447` on 6 df,
  in the same family as the committed transitions (2163 / 262 / 396 / 826 / …).
  **Per #612's instruction: the full-law residual remains structured.** One

## 4. Step 2 housekeeping, and one clean positive

* **Cross-size covariance.** Measured, not assumed: batch-level correlation
  between 290 and 725 is `0.180` against a noise floor of `0.100` (100 batches),
  below the 3× threshold, so `S_290 + S_725` stands. The three productions use
  distinct seeds (`2026105003 / 2026105004 / 2026105011`); matching batch IDs
  would not have established coupling.
* **The label crossing (#609 item 2) is real.** `v_5(725) = 2` **and** the spin-0
  combination is an interpolation (`cos 4θ = +0.495854 / −0.578068`, weights
  `0.53828 / 0.46172`, both in `[0,1]`) — so 725 is the first size in the tree
  where 5-adic valuation and interpolation status disagree, exactly as #609
  designed. The new rung is not observationally identical to the production-campaign
  negative control. This is the one thing the block settles cleanly.

## 5. A numerical discrepancy that must be on the record

#582's reproduction control does **not** pass bitwise in this environment. It is
quantified and it does not propagate, but it is reported rather than hidden:

* the **displacement vectors are bit-identical** (difference exactly `0.0` on
  all nine components for 65→130) — the data path is exact;
* the affine **residuals** differ by up to `1.1e-08` (relative `1.4e-05`), and
  the chi-square statistic by `2.37` out of `4.357e+05` (`5.4e-06` relative);
* the covariance is badly conditioned (`cond = 1.5e+10` for 65→130, rank 9 from
  100 batches), so the mpmath pseudo-inverse at `relative_tolerance = 1e-12` is
  where the drift enters. The most likely cause is an mpmath version difference
  from the environment that generated `results/wasserstein-shape-flow/latest.json`.
* Downstream agreement: amplitudes `~1e-06` relative, `omega` `8.8e-08`,
  `lambda` `6.0e-07`, `|Δg| <= 4.3e-06`. All three to four orders of magnitude
  below the 2.7 % effect being tested, and every published ratio in §1
  reproduces to five digits.

Also noted: `p582_amplitude_law.reproduction_control` always compares against
the **spin0** published statistics, so its `equal`-weighting row is not a
meaningful reproduction test. That is pre-existing, not introduced here.

## 6. Decision

1. **Step 1 passes.** The chart identity is real, closes at 3e-13, and the
   chart term carries 95–97 % of the old excess on the primary weighting.
2. **The practical first-amplitude forecast is supported under the declared
   primary weighting** (3 se interval wholly inside the ±5 % band) — and is
   **not robust**: the orientation-weighting systematic is 12 %, and the point
   forecast is missed by 5.6 nominal se.
3. **The curvature comparison is retired as a second-scale test.** `P′` and the
   1.547 target are 0.7 % apart; the run cannot and does not separate them. What
   survives is a ~4 % curvature misfit that the one-exponent law does not
   explain.
4. **Stop here**, per #612: no larger N, no free second exponent, no quantile
   window chosen after the score, no additional production. The old artifacts
   are preserved; nothing was overwritten.
5. **The full-law residual remains structured.** The next thing that would
   actually discriminate is a changed readout, not another rung.
