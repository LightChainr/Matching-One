# Interchiral singlet V_<1,3>: a real spin-4 descendant, but matching-even under the fusion involution

Status: selection-rule note for the diagonal singlet competitor below x=21/4.

## 1. The dangerous singlet family is physically present

The generic-Q Potts space of states contains

\[
\bigoplus_{s\ge1}\mathscr R_{\langle1,s\rangle}\otimes[],
\]

so all diagonal degenerate modules `R_<1,s>` are Potts singlets.  They combine into the interchiral identity representation generated by the degenerate singlet field `V_<1,2>`.

At percolation,

\[
V_{\langle1,3\rangle}:\qquad h=\bar h=1/3,\quad x=2/3.
\]

Its level-3 null relation does **not** remove all level-4 quasiprimary directions.  `scripts/v13_level4_spin4_candidate.py` checks explicitly that

\[
Q_4^{(1,3)}=
-4L_{-4}-15L_{-3}L_{-1}+10L_{-2}^2
\]

satisfies `L1 Q4=0` and is nonzero modulo the level-3 null descendant.

The bulk chiral/conjugate field has

\[
x=2/3+4=14/3,\qquad s=\pm4.
\]

If it appeared in the matching-odd central residual it would produce

\[
\Delta M\sim L^{-8/3}=N^{-4/3},
\]

and a root bias

\[
\Delta p^*\sim L^{-41/12}.
\]

Therefore it is a more serious symmetry competitor than the non-singlet formal `x=17/4` primary.

## 2. Interchiral fusion gives a parity selection rule

For the Potts CFT the singlet degenerate field `V_<1,2>` extends the Virasoro algebra to the interchiral algebra.  Its standard degenerate fusion includes

\[
V_{\langle1,2\rangle}\times V_{\langle1,2\rangle}
\simeq V_{\langle1,1\rangle}+V_{\langle1,3\rangle}.
\]

Assume the scaling-limit matching map `T` is

1. an involution, `T^2=1`;
2. an automorphism of the local OPE/fusion algebra;
3. compatible with the interchiral algebra.

Choose `V_<1,2>` in an involution eigenbasis,

\[
T V_{\langle1,2\rangle}=\tau V_{\langle1,2\rangle},\qquad \tau=\pm1.
\]

Then its self-OPE has parity

\[
\tau^2=+1.
\]

The identity term is even, and—barring a degeneracy that lets `T` rotate equal-quantum-number OPE channels—the `V_<1,3>` contribution must also be matching-even:

\[
\boxed{T V_{\langle1,3\rangle}=+V_{\langle1,3\rangle}.}
\]

If `T` commutes with Virasoro generators, every ordinary Virasoro descendant of `V_<1,3>` inherits the same even parity.  In particular

\[
\boxed{\eta(Q_4^{(1,3)})=+1.}
\]

Thus the `x=14/3` spin-4 field is allowed in the matching-even angular sector `S`, but is projected out of the central matching-odd sector `D=M/2`.

## 3. Recursive parity of the diagonal interchiral tower

The degenerate fusion

\[
V_{\langle1,2\rangle}\times V_{\langle1,s\rangle}
\simeq V_{\langle1,s-1\rangle}+V_{\langle1,s+1\rangle}
\]

implies, under the same no-degenerate-rotation assumption,

\[
\eta_{s+1}=\tau\eta_s,\qquad \eta_1=+1.
\]

Hence

```text
odd s  : eta_s = +1
even s : eta_s = tau
```

independently of whether the generator `V_<1,2>` itself is even or odd.

This makes the whole odd-s diagonal singlet family matching-even.

## 4. Numerical implication for the two-spin-4 model

The current center hierarchy can now be refined:

### matching-even S

Potential square-spin-4 contributions include

- dimension 4 sector: `P4[S] ~ N^-1` (observed leading behavior);
- `V_<1,3>` level-4 sector: `~N^-4/3`, a natural subleading correction if its lattice coupling is nonzero;
- further even interchiral descendants.

Thus a mild radial drift of the scaled `N*P4[S]` amplitude is not surprising and does not by itself threaten the leading `x=4` term.

### matching-odd D

The `V_<1,3>` descendant should be absent at the center.  The thermal family is matching-odd under the commuting-involution hypothesis, so its level-4 field at `x=21/4` remains the leading identified singlet spin-4 candidate for `D`.
