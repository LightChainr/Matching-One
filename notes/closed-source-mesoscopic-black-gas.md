# The dilute Sstar sector remains an iid black gas when its mean diverges

**Theorem.** Let `N=25k²` and use either square Gaussian quotient
generated by `(5k,0)` or `(4k,3k)`. Let `m=m_N -> infinity` with

\[
 \frac{N}{m^4}\longrightarrow0,
 \tag{1}
\]

while `lambda_N=N/m²` may tend to infinity. For the projected Sstar
weight

\[
 w(A)=h^{K(A)}m^{-B_{mix}(A)+2C_B(A)-r(A)},
 \tag{2}
\]

take, uniformly for `|s|<=S`,

\[
 h=h_c e^{s/N},\qquad h_c=(1-m^{-2})^{-1},\qquad
 x=h/m^2,qquad p_x=x/(1+x).
 \tag{3}
\]

Then the full rank-zero partition and its conditional occupation law
satisfy, for every fixed M,

\[
 \boxed{Z_0(h,m)=(1+x)^N
  \left[1+O_S\!\left(\frac{N}{m^4}+N^{-M}\right)\right],}
 \tag{4}
\]

\[
 \boxed{d_{TV}\bigl(\mathcal L(A\mid r=0),
             \operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_x)^{\otimes N}\bigr)
 =O_S\!\left(\frac{N}{m^4}+N^{-M}\right).}
 \tag{5}
\]

Moreover `Np_x=lambda_N+O_S(m^-2)`, so

\[
 d_{TV}\bigl(\mathcal L(K\mid r=0),
              \operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_N)\bigr)
 =O_S\!\left(\frac{N}{m^4}+rac1{m^2}+N^{-M}\right).
 \tag{6}
\]

Thus if lambda diverges, `K/lambda ->1` in conditional probability:
the low phase is a mesoscopic dilute cloud, not the empty state.

This extends only the dilute side of
[the bounded-lambda theorem](closed-source-poisson-double-scaling.md).
It does not prove the matching rank-two defect estimate, the pooled
root, or original U in this larger regime. No enumeration, simulation,
old score or fitted contour constant enters.

## 1. A cutoff that absorbs the old volume exponential

Let `ell_N` be the shortest nonzero Manhattan period. It equals `5k`
for the axis quotient and `7k` for the tilted quotient. Put

\[
 A_N=\frac{108N}{m^2(1-3/m)},\qquad a=\log(m/3),
\]

and choose the integer cutoff

\[
 R=\max\left\{16,
 \left\lceil\frac{A_N+(M+2)\log N+2\log m+10}{a}\right\rceil
 \right\}.
 \tag{7}
\]

Condition (1) gives

\[
 \frac{R}{\ell_N}\to0,qquad
 \frac{R}{m^2}\to0,qquad
 \frac{R}{N}\to0.
 \tag{8}
\]

Indeed the potentially largest term is
`A_N/a=O(N/(m² log m))`; divided by `sqrt(N)` it is
`O(sqrt(N/m⁴)/log m)=o(1)`. The logarithmic terms are smaller.

The existing honest-torus contour bound is

\[
 Z(\text{some contour length}\ge R)
 \le 2M_h e^{A_N}B_R,qquad
 B_R=\frac{4Nm^2}{3(1-3/m)}(3/m)^R,
 \tag{9}
\]

where this is an **unnormalized** inequality and
`M_h=max(1,h^N)`. The choice (7) makes the right side, after division
by a partition of order `(1+x)^N`, `O_S(N^{-M})`; section 4 supplies
that denominator explicitly. Thus the old `exp(A_N)` has not been
deleted. It is paid for by R, which remains below the systole.

The chart obeys, for large N,

\[
 |\log h|\le 2/m^2+S/N.
 \tag{10}
\]

Together with (8), this makes the area cost of every short contour
subexponential in its perimeter in the precise sense used next.

## 2. Connected black polymers, including holes

On the event that every resolved contour has length below R, every
rank-zero occupied NN component lifts to a finite connected subset C
of the square lattice. Let `k=|C|`, let b be its complete mixed-edge
perimeter, and let l be the length of its outer boundary. Its exact
activity in (2) is

\[
 \zeta(C)=h^k m^{2-b}.
 \tag{11}
\]

Different black components are vertex-disjoint and non-NN-adjacent;
their activities multiply. A singleton has `l=b=4` and activity
`x=h/m²`. Every nonsingleton has `l>=6`.

The hole issue can be retained rather than filled or ignored. A lifted
outer contour of length l encloses at most `V_l<=c_0 l²` sites. If its
hole contours have lengths `n_1,...,n_j`, then
`b=l+sum n_i`. Fixing the oriented outer contour and summing possible
hole families, while dropping disjointness and consistency, gives

\[
 \sum_{C:\,\partial_{out}C=\gamma}\zeta(C)
 \le m^{2-l}\exp\left{
 c_0l^2|\log h|+
 \frac{108c_0l^2}{m^4(1-3/m)}\right}.
 \tag{12}

To obtain the second term, root a possible hole walk at one of
`O(V_l)` dual positions. The number of length-n nonbacktracking walks
is at most `4V_l 3^(n-1)`, and

\[
 \sum_{n\ge4}4V_l3^{n-1}m^{-n}
 =\frac{108V_l}{m^4(1-3/m)}.
\]

The product over arbitrary candidate holes is at most the exponential
of this sum. Given consistent outer and hole cut curves and the choice
that the region next to the outer curve is black, the component is
fixed; there is no uncounted `2^(area)` interior choice. Other black
islands inside a white hole are separate polymers and are handled by
the component gas. This is why (12) does not silently discard nested
occupation patterns.

Let

\[
 \eta_N=c_1R\left(2/m^2+S/N+m^{-4}ight)=o(1).
\]

For l<R, the exponential in (12) is at most `exp(eta_N l)`.
There are at most `4N3^(l-1)` rooted oriented outer curves. For large
N, absorb `3 exp(eta_N)` into 4. The total activity of all nonsingleton
short components is therefore

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal A_{ns}:=\sum_{C:\,|C|\ge2,\ l(C)<R}\zeta(C)
 \le \frac{4N}{3}m^2\sum_{l\ge6}(4/m)^l
 =O_S(N/m^4).}
 \tag{13}

This is the required aggregate bound. It is stronger than assigning
an `exp(O(N/m²))` factor to all short contours and is valid even when
`N/m²` diverges. It also avoids a full-polymer KP condition: only the
summed surplus activity relative to the singleton gas is used.

## 3. Relative normalization by the same independent-set gas

Let Zind be the partition containing singleton black components only.
It is the NN hard-core gas of activity x. Under iid Bernoulli(p_x),

\[
 Z_{ind}=(1+x)^N\Pr(\text{no occupied NN edge}).
\]

A union bound over the 2N NN edges gives

\[
 (1+x)^N(1-2Np_x^2)\le Z_{ind}\le(1+x)^N.
 \tag{14}

At s=0, `p_x=1/m²` exactly. More generally
`p_x=[1+(m²-1)e^{-s/N}]^{-1}`, so uniformly on the chart
`Np_x²=O_S(N/m⁴)=o(1)`.

Now sum any compatible collection P of nonsingleton components. Once
P is fixed, the allowed singleton partition is at most Zind: removing
sites and imposing additional exclusions cannot increase a positive
hard-core partition. Dropping all compatibility constraints between
the nonsingleton components consequently gives the exact relative
bound

\[
 Z_{ind}\le Z_{0,good}
 \le Z_{ind}\sum_P\prod_{C\in P}\zeta(C)
 \le Z_{ind}\exp(\mathcal A_{ns}).
 \tag{15}

This is the normalization step that a bare contour product misses.
The surplus factor multiplies the same independent-set gas; it is not
an additional volume partition `exp(A_N)`.

Equations (13)-(15) prove

\[
 Z_{0,good}=(1+x)^N[1+O_S(N/m^4)].
 \tag{16}

The comparison is two-sided, despite the fact that hard-core exclusion
and nonsingleton rewards have opposite effects relative to unrestricted
iid particles.

## 4. Long contours are small relative to the dilute partition

The lower bound in (14) is eventually at least `(1+x)^N/2`.
Furthermore

\[
 M_h/(1+x)^N\le e^S.
 \tag{17}

If h<=1 this is immediate. If h>=1, the ratio is one at h=h_c,
and its log derivative along `h=h_c e^{s/N}` is bounded by one, so
its Nth power changes by at most `e^|s|`.

Apply the unnormalized bound (9) to the rank-zero bad subensemble and
divide by `Z0,good>=Zind`. The explicit cutoff gives

\[
 \frac{Z_{0,bad}}{Z_{0,good}}
 \le 4e^S e^{A_N}B_R=O_S(N^{-M}).
 \tag{18}

Thus `Z0=Z0,good[1+O_S(N^-M)]`; combining with (16) proves (4).
This step also shows why normalizing a bad-contour estimate by the
empty configuration would be wasteful when lambda diverges.

The absolute scale is transparent. Since

\[
 N\log(1+x)=N\log h_c+s/m^2+O_S((Nm^2)^{-1}),
\]

equation (4) gives

\[
 \log Z_0=N\log h_c+s/m^2
 +O_S(N/m^4+N^{-M})=\lambda_N+o(1).
 \tag{19}

The last equality uses `N log h_c=lambda_N+O(N/m⁴)`.
The dilute partition may therefore diverge like `exp(lambda_N)`; it
is its **relative** error that vanishes.

For comparison only, the single full-state weight is
`h^N=h_c^N e^s`. Equation (19) makes its ratio to Z0 asymptotic to
`e^s`. Promoting that observation to the full rank-two sector requires
a separate uniform defect bound and is not part of this theorem.

## 5. Total-variation and Poisson consequences

Conditioned on containing only singleton components, the occupation
law is exactly iid Bernoulli(p_x) conditioned on no adjacent occupied
pair. Its total variation distance from unconditioned iid is the iid
probability of violating that event, at most `2Np_x²`.

Under the good rank-zero law, the probability of any nonsingleton
component is at most `exp(A_ns)-1=O(N/m⁴)`, by (15). Under the full
rank-zero law, the probability of a long contour is `O(N^-M)`, by
(18). The triangle inequality proves (5).

Data processing transfers this bound to K. Le Cam's inequality gives
`dTV(Binomial(N,p_x),Poisson(Np_x))<=Np_x²`. Finally

\[
 Np_x-\lambda_N=\int_0^s p_x(t)(1-p_x(t))dt=O_S(m^{-2}),
\]

and two Poisson laws can be coupled within the absolute difference of
their means. This proves (6), including when lambda_N diverges. In that
case Poisson concentration plus the vanishing total-variation error
gives `K/lambda_N ->1` in probability, without claiming convergence
to a fixed limiting distribution.

## Boundary and unproved strengthening

The proven conclusions are (4)-(6) for the rank-zero/dilute sector on
the two specified growing Gaussian quotients. They require
`N/m⁴->0`, not bounded `N/m²`; the cutoff is allowed to grow but remains
strictly below the systole.

It remains conjectural in this note that the complete rank-two sector
is uniformly the single full state throughout this larger window.
Without that second relative polymer estimate one cannot yet infer the
full two-phase law, pooled matching root, denominator, or suppression
of original U. The common leading factor `h_c^N` and the full/dilute
ratio `e^s` identify the right coexistence chart, but do not substitute
for those missing estimates.
