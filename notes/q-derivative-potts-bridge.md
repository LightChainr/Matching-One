# Potts-Q derivative bridge for the matching scaling function

Status: theoretical program. The purpose is to replace ad-hoc special-function guessing by an operator-level identification of the universal cluster-number scaling function.

## 1. Cluster number is a Q derivative, not an arbitrary observable

For bond percolation on a finite graph `G=(V,E)`, write the normalized random-cluster partition function

\[
Z_G(Q,p)=\sum_{A\subseteq E}
 p^{|A|}(1-p)^{|E|-|A|}Q^{k(A)},
\]

where `k(A)` is the number of connected components, with whatever isolated-vertex convention is used consistently.

At `Q=1`,

\[
Z_G(1,p)=1.
\]

Therefore

\[
\left.\partial_Q Z_G(Q,p)\right|_{Q=1}
=\langle k\rangle_p,
\]

and because `Z=1` there,

\[
\left.\partial_Q\log Z_G(Q,p)\right|_{Q=1}
=\langle k\rangle_p.
\]

Higher `Q` derivatives generate cluster-number cumulants. This is exactly the route used in the Potts/Coulomb-gas derivations of the universal excess cluster number on a torus (Kleban and Ziff, 1998).

For site percolation one needs the dilute/site-cluster analogue rather than the ordinary edge random-cluster model, but the continuum universality statement is the same object after nonuniversal metric normalization.

## 2. Near-critical finite-size free energy

Let the universal finite-size part of the Potts free energy on a torus of fixed modulus `tau` be schematically

\[
F_{\rm univ}(Q,t,L,\tau)
=\mathcal F_Q(x,\tau)+\text{irrelevant corrections},
\qquad x=u_t(Q,t)L^{y_t(Q)}.
\]

At `Q=1`, the `Q` derivative produces the universal finite-size cluster-number scaling function, modulo chain-rule contributions associated with the `Q` dependence of the critical manifold and the thermal normalization:

\[
f(z,\tau)
\sim
\left.\partial_Q\mathcal F_Q(x,\tau)\right|_{Q=1}
+\text{coordinate terms}.
\]

This caution matters. The derivative defining the lattice cluster number is taken at fixed microscopic probability/coupling, not at fixed scaling coordinate `x`. Therefore `partial_Q p_c(Q)`, `partial_Q y_t(Q)`, and `partial_Q b(Q)` can contribute when translating between lattice and continuum coordinates.

The correct research target is an operator identity with these chain-rule pieces controlled, not the naive equation `f=partial_Q F` with every other variable held fixed.

## 3. Matching odd function as thermal antisymmetrization

The finite matching relation gives the scaling limit

\[
\mathcal M(z,\tau)=f(z,\tau)-f(-z,\tau).
\]

Hence

\[
\mathcal M(-z,\tau)=-\mathcal M(z,\tau).
\]

The even part of the cluster-number scaling function is projected out. This makes the matching observable unusually clean from the field-theory point of view: it probes only the thermal-odd sector of the `Q`-derivative free energy.

Expand

\[
\mathcal M(z)=m_1z+m_3z^3+m_5z^5+\cdots.
\]

Then

\[
\kappa_3=\frac{\mathcal M'''(0)}{\mathcal M'(0)^3}
=\frac{6m_3}{m_1^3},
\]

and similarly for higher derivative ratios. These are invariant under the linear rescaling `z -> c z`.

## 4. Interpretation as mixed thermal / cluster-fugacity response

At the critical point, derivatives with respect to the thermal coupling insert the thermal operator `epsilon`. A derivative with respect to `Q` changes the cluster fugacity and, in the `Q -> 1` continuation, probes the logarithmic Potts/percolation sector.

Schematically,

\[
\mathcal M'(0)
\leftrightarrow
\partial_Q\partial_t F_{\rm torus}|_{Q=1,t=0},
\]

\[
\mathcal M'''(0)
\leftrightarrow
\partial_Q\partial_t^3 F_{\rm torus}|_{Q=1,t=0},
\]

up to the coordinate terms discussed above.

Thus `kappa_3` is not merely a ratio of numerical derivatives. It is a normalized ratio of integrated torus response functions involving the thermal operator and the `Q`-derivative/logarithmic sector.

This suggests a concrete LCFT program:

1. identify the operator generated by `partial_Q` at `Q=1` in the torus partition function;
2. write the conformal-perturbation expansion in the thermal field;
3. compute or bootstrap the one- and three-thermal-insertion coefficients entering `m_1` and `m_3`;
4. form the normalization-independent ratio;
5. compare with the numerical `kappa_3` limit before guessing a rational value.

The percolation LCFT literature already identifies logarithmic mixing in the `Q -> 1` Potts limit, including mixing of the energy field with cluster-creating fields (Vasseur, Jacobsen, Saleur 2012; Vasseur, Jacobsen 2014). This makes an operator-level interpretation plausible, though no claim is made here that the required torus integrated correlators are presently known in closed form.

## 5. Why the long cylinder may be the first solvable geometry

The thermally perturbed scaling `Q`-state Potts theories for `Q <= 4` are integrable and have known exact scattering descriptions. Finite-volume ground-state energies have been studied with thermodynamic Bethe ansatz / nonlinear integral equations (Dorey, Pocklington, Tateo 2002 and related work).

A full square torus requires the finite-temperature trace over the whole spectrum and is substantially harder than a ground-state cylinder calculation.

Therefore use aspect ratio as a controlled bridge.

Let a torus have dimensions `L x R` with `r=R/L`. Define

\[
\kappa_3(r)
\]

for the matching scaling function at fixed modulus `tau=i r`.

Program:

1. simulate or transfer-matrix compute `kappa_3(r)` for increasing `r`;
2. extrapolate `r -> infinity` to the long-cylinder limit;
3. independently derive the `Q`-derivative thermal response from the finite-volume integrable Potts theory;
4. compare the cylinder limit before attempting the full square-torus value;
5. if successful, work back toward finite `r` using excited-state TBA / spectral sums.

The cylinder value need not equal the square-torus value: `kappa_3` is shape-dependent. The point is to validate the field-theory identification in a geometry for which integrability gives more leverage.

## 6. Critical integrable controls

At exactly criticality, periodic bond percolation on the square lattice and site percolation on the triangular lattice have integrable lattice descriptions and torus partition functions in the same `c=0` universality class.

This provides a hierarchy of tests:

### Level A — exact critical torus

Recover known universal excess-cluster quantities from the `Q` derivative of the critical torus partition function.

### Level B — first thermal response

Match the numerically measured `mathcal M'(0)` after fixing the metric factor from one observable.

### Level C — invariant ratio

Predict or reproduce

\[
\kappa_3=\frac{\mathcal M'''(0)}{\mathcal M'(0)^3}.
\]

Level C is the first stage where the candidate `-5/3` becomes theoretically meaningful.

## 7. A more useful object than isolated derivatives

Define the normalized even density

\[
\rho(z)=\frac12\mathcal M'(z).
\]

If `mathcal M(z)` is monotone from `-1` to `+1` in the scaling window, then

\[
\rho(z)\ge0,
\qquad
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\rho(z)\,dz=1.
\]

Thus the derivative of the odd matching crossover can be viewed as a universal threshold-density profile. Its width is nonuniversal under `z` rescaling, but standardized shape quantities are universal.

For example,

\[
\kappa_3
=\frac{\rho''(0)}{4\rho(0)^3}.
\]

Rather than estimating only `kappa_3`, reconstruct the full standardized profile

\[
\tilde\rho(x)
\]

from Newman-Ziff data and compare it across square bond, square site, union-jack site, and other realizations at the same torus modulus. A whole function provides far stronger evidence for universality or a candidate analytic form than one rational-looking number.

## 8. Candidate analytic-family tests should be shape-level, not constant-level

Only after a high-precision standardized `rho(x)` is available should one compare candidate families such as logistic/sech-type, generalized normal, Painleve/Fredholm-derived profiles, or an integrable-TBA numerical profile.

A candidate must predict simultaneously:

- `kappa_3`;
- `kappa_5`;
- tail behavior;
- the full central shape;
- aspect-ratio dependence.

Fitting one parameter to make `kappa_3=-5/3` is not evidence.

## 9. Immediate numerical outputs to preserve

The server pipeline should, whenever possible, retain enough microcanonical information to reconstruct the full curve later rather than storing only roots.

For every size and geometry preserve:

- microcanonical matching-channel means by occupation count;
- covariance between channels;
- enough metadata to redo binomial convolution at arbitrary precision;
- the exact geometry period matrix;
- sample counts / RNG counter ranges;
- raw first few thermal derivatives or the data needed to recompute them.

This avoids having to rerun a large GPU campaign when a new invariant or field-theory comparison is proposed.
