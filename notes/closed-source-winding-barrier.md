# Exact winding barrier of the closed source

**Result.** On a nondegenerate square torus `Z^2/Lambda`, put
`ell1(Lambda)=min_(0!=lambda in Lambda) ||lambda||_1`. For the already
fixed closed source, the minimum rank-one cost is exactly

```text
min_(r=1) g = 2 ell1(Lambda)-1.                       (1)
```

The lower bound survives alternating-face dual intersections and any
number of occupied components. A shortest-period occupied staircase cycle
attains it. For Gaussian periods `(a,b),(-b,a)` with `a>=b>=0`,
`ell1=a+b`: the N25 axis and tilted barriers are respectively 9 and 13.
Uniform dilation by an integer k changes them to `10k-1` and `14k-1`.

This is a finite-geometric, pre-numerical prediction of the fixed source.
The note starts at `359bde9b`; it uses no finite-coupling scores, fitted
curves, Monte Carlo or enumeration.

## 1. Cost, scope and the sharper component bound

Use the ordinary occupied NN graph, the established vacant matching
convention, and geometric edge/face incidences. Write C_B for the number
of occupied components, Bmix for NN bonds with unlike endpoints, and r
for the ambient H1-image rank of the occupied graph. The identities in
[the action note](decimation-closed-cluster-gas-action.md) give

```text
g = 2N+1-K-S* = 2K-(beta1+beta_null)
  = 4K-2Bocc-2C_B+r = Bmix-2C_B+r.                   (2)
```

Assume an injective 3x3 stencil, which is a sufficient nonalias condition;
Gaussian area N>8 suffices. In particular the graph has four distinct NN
neighbors per vertex and ell1>=3. The proof concerns this fixed embedded
graph, without suppressing geometric gain incidences in a smaller alias
quotient. No checkerboard parity assumption is needed.

If r=1, let e>=1 be the number of occupied components with nonzero ambient
image and c0 the number with zero image. Then C_B=e+c0. In fact

```text
g >= e(2 ell1-2)+2c0+1
  = 2 ell1-1 + (e-1)(2 ell1-2)+2c0.                 (3)
```

Thus extra occupied components cannot reduce the single-carrier barrier.
Below, a zero-image component may contain many contractible loops; it is
not assumed to be a tree or a disk.

## 2. Boundaries without a hidden dual-crossing assumption

For each occupied NN component form its digital regular neighborhood R:
take the unit cells centered on its occupied sites, join cells across
occupied NN edges, and smooth the corners. At an alternating original
face, the two diagonally occupied cells must remain separated. Equivalently,
resolve the four-valent cut-dual vertex into the two arcs rounding the
occupied corners. This is the occupied-NN/vacant-matching convention.
It produces disjoint embedded boundary curves, even if their unsmoothed
dual walks share a dual vertex.

R has the same connectedness and ambient homology image as the occupied
graph. The corner smoothing may fill all-occupied elementary faces, but
those are contractible two-cells and do not change the ambient image.
Every mixed NN bond contributes precisely one edge to the cut-dual walks,
and every such edge is used once. Assign a boundary curve its integer
dual-step length, before the arbitrarily small corner smoothing. The sum
of these lengths is exactly the component's mixed-edge count. A lift of
a boundary walk with period displacement lambda has length at least
`||lambda||_1`; smoothing changes neither that displacement nor the edge
accounting.

Consider an occupied component whose ambient rank is one. Its regular
neighborhood is a connected proper orientable subsurface of the torus.
It cannot have genus one: the two handle cycles would have nonzero
intersection number in the torus and would generate ambient rank two.
It is therefore genus zero. Its first homology is generated by boundary
curves, so at least one boundary curve is essential. Disjoint essential
simple curves in a torus have a common primitive direction; primitive
here means primitive in Lambda, not coprime Cartesian coordinates.
Their oriented total homology is zero, as they bound R. Consequently
there are at least two essential boundary curves, with opposing
orientations. Each costs at least ell1 mixed edges, giving

```text
Bmix(component) >= 2 ell1.                          (4)
```

This does not assume a single simple occupied cycle or a single annular
component. Holes only add boundaries. Distinct essential components have
disjoint neighborhoods; alternatively, total rank one already forces
their winding images to have the same real direction. Each component
separately obeys (4).

For a zero-image occupied component, every lift component is finite and
projects bijectively onto the original one. Indeed all closed graph walks
have zero period displacement, so path lifting is independent of the
chosen path. A finite nonempty subset of the square lattice has at least
four outgoing edges: one in each signed coordinate direction, at its
extrema. Every outgoing edge here goes to a vacant site; an occupied
neighbor would belong to the same lifted component. Hence

```text
Bmix(component) >= 4.                               (5)
```

Every mixed edge has exactly one occupied endpoint, so component boundary
counts add without duplication. Combining (4)--(5) yields
`Bmix>=2e ell1+4c0`; substitution in (2) proves (3) and the lower half of
(1). If the occupied winding direction is the primitive lambda, the same
argument sharpens ell1 in (3) to `||lambda||_1`.

## 3. A shortest-period staircase attains the bound

Choose a nonzero period lambda minimizing Manhattan length ell1, and any
monotone NN path from 0 to lambda in the covering square lattice. Project
it to the torus. Its ell1 successive vertices are distinct before the
endpoint closes: a repetition would give a nonzero period represented by
a shorter subpath. Thus it is a simple essential cycle of length ell1.

It has no additional NN chord. A chord between nonconsecutive vertices
would split the cycle into two closed walks, each of length less than
ell1. Neither can have a nonzero period, by minimality of ell1; their
oriented sum is the original cycle, contradicting its nonzero period.
For ell1=3 there are no nonconsecutive vertices to consider.

Occupy exactly the vertices of this cycle and no others. The induced
occupied graph therefore has

```text
K=ell1,   Bocc=ell1,   C_B=1,   beta1=r=1,
Bmix=2ell1,   g=2ell1-1.                            (6)
```

This is a one-site-thick staircase stripe, with a regular annular
neighborhood. It proves attainability without searching configurations.
It need not exhaust the minimizers: on an axis LxL torus, every straight
stripe of width 1 through L-1 also has two length-L boundaries and the
same cost 2L-1. Equality in the lower-bound proof requires one essential
occupied component, no other occupied components, and exactly two
shortest essential boundaries with no additional boundary length.

## 4. Gaussian periods and the dilation prediction

Let `Lambda=(a+ib)Z[i]`, with integers `a>=b>=0`, not both zero. The unit
multipliers give periods of Manhattan length a+b. Every nonunit Gaussian
integer z has Euclidean norm at least sqrt(2), and therefore

```text
||(a+ib)z||_1 >= ||(a+ib)z||_2
              >= sqrt(2(a^2+b^2)) >= a+b.
```

So ell1=a+b for every such Gaussian period lattice; the nonalias scope is
only needed for the stated graph theorem. The shortest staircase can be
chosen as a east steps followed by b north steps, with the endpoint
identified through the period (a,b).

| Period generator | Area | ell1 | Exact minimum rank-one g |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | 25 | 5 | 9 |
| 4+3i | 25 | 7 | 13 |
| 5k | 25k^2 | 5k | 10k-1 |
| k(4+3i) | 25k^2 | 7k | 14k-1 |

More generally `ell1(k Lambda)=k ell1(Lambda)`, so the barrier is
`2k ell1(Lambda)-1`. This is a linear-size cost, despite the area growing
quadratically. The extra tilted-versus-axis cost in this pair is 4k.

## 5. The finite strong-coupling statement that follows

At fixed positive h, use the already frozen weights `h^K m^(-g)` with
`m=exp(t)`. Let Z1 be their unnormalized rank-one sum. Since Bmix is even,
all rank-one g values are odd. Equation (1) therefore gives

```text
Z1(h,m) = m^(-(2ell1-1)) [P_Lambda(h)+O(m^(-2))],
P_Lambda(h) = sum_(r=1,g=2ell1-1) h^K > 0.           (7)
```

This is a finite polynomial asymptotic, uniform for h in a compact subset
of (0,infinity). It predicts the first nonzero rank-one source power at
larger sizes before their histograms are counted; it does not calculate
the degeneracy polynomial P. On the dilated equal-area pair the ratio of
rank-one partition sums is a positive h-dependent prefactor times
`m^(-4k)[1+O(m^(-2))]`.

Normalization and a chosen root may subsequently be inserted using the
separate finite-coupling theory. The barrier alone supplies no sign for a
thermal derivative or for root/slope-normalized U: those also read the
h-dependence and multiplicities of the minimizing states. No claim about
a continuum exponent, an infinite-volume transition or the order of
large-size and large-coupling limits is needed for (1)--(7).
