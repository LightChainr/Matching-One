# A microscopic deformation with zero linear response and a nontrivial global-U endpoint

## Result

Consider the **ordinary NN square-site graph**, without adding diagonals,
on an honest bipartite torus. Occupy its two checkerboard sublattices with

\[
p_A=s+(1-s)p,\qquad p_B=p,\qquad 0\le s\le1.
\]

Use exactly the existing global observable, with the **parent** area N,
the declared orientation projector, and the pooled matching root at each s:

\[
Q=\overline{\langle q\rangle},\quad Y=\mathcal P_4\langle E\rangle,
\quad q=r-1,\quad E=q^2,\quad Q(p_0(s),s)=0,
\qquad U_N(s)=\frac{N^{13/8}}2\frac{Y_p}{Q_p}\bigg|_{p_0(s)}.
\]

The thermal derivative holds **s** fixed. Then two exact statements coexist:

\[
\boxed{U_N'(0)=0},\qquad
\boxed{U_N(1)=2^{13/8}U_{N/2}(0)}.
\]

The child in the second equation is the square quotient with periods
divided by1+i, including the corresponding orientation map. The numerical
endpoint differs substantially from the native parent on all four available
old baseline pairs. Thus a microscopic deformation can be invisible to
every first-order test of this U at the homogeneous point while changing
the same U at finite strength. This is a concrete nonlinear-transmission
example, not a repair of the failed lag1 source or identification of H4's
continuum field.

## Known model, new finite-observer use

Sublattice-selective square-site percolation and its fully occupied
sublattice reduction are already in the literature. Wattendorff and Wessel
describe the square NN+NNN endpoint, its threshold1−pc, and an adapted
Newman–Ziff method in [Phys. Rev. E109,044108 / arXiv:2401.12821](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12821).
Their phase-diagram and universality results are not a derivation of the
present finite-U identity. We use the known endpoint to transport the
repository's ambient-homology observer, the pooled root and its normalized
thermal derivative, with no continuum approximation.

The repository's [earlier staggered family](c4-self-matching-tangent-improvement.md)
is on a different graph: the self-matching triangulation with degree8/4
sublattices. Here both sublattices have degree4 and translation exchanges
them. Its first-order staggered null and the endpoint map must not be
imported from that inequivalent-sublattice control.

## Why the endpoint preserves ambient homology

Let the torus be C/Λ with Λ contained in the even-parity sublattice
(1+i)Z[i]. At s=1 every A site is present. A nonconstant occupied NN closed
walk alternates A/B. Remove its A vertices: every resulting B–B step is one
of ±2,±2i,±1±i, and its displacement is unchanged. Conversely each such
matching step between occupied B sites can be replaced by a length-two
path through an occupied A site. These two operations preserve the lifted
period displacement of every closed walk.

After an affine translation of the B coset and division by1+i, the eight
steps are precisely the NN+NNN square matching graph. The walks need not be
simple; their ambient homology image is generated by their displacements.
Isolated A sites contribute no essential cycles. Therefore the ambient
image, not merely ordinary connectivity, transports to the matching child.
Use lifted multiedges and the existing digital conventions; no graph-only
crossing simplification or small-quotient alias is being asserted.

Applying the exact finite Alexander matching complement on the child gives
for each directly mapped geometry

\[
q_{\Lambda}(p,1)=-q_{\Lambda/(1+i)}(1-p,0),\qquad
E_{\Lambda}(p,1)=E_{\Lambda/(1+i)}(1-p,0).
\]

Here q,E denote expectations; the occupied child's complement is ordinary
square-site percolation. In particular

\[
p_{0,\Lambda}(1)=1-p_{0,\Lambda/(1+i)}(0),\quad
Q_{p,\Lambda}(p,1)=Q_{p,child}(1-p,0).
\]

The E thermal derivative changes sign under p↦1−p. Dividing periods by1+i
rotates the lattice by−π/4, so cos4θ and its orientation difference also
change sign. The two signs cancel in Y_p, giving the **positive** endpoint
factor2^(13/8) from the parent/child area normalization. Reordering or
reflecting the child to its canonical orientation table changes numerator
and projector consistently; it does not supply another physical minus.

This is why the chosen saturation parameterization matters. If one instead
holds h fixed in p_A=p+h,p_B=p−h, the endpoint thermal derivative also
differentiates the fully occupied sublattice. The endpoint reduction alone
does not determine that derivative, so the simple identity above must not
be used for that thermal convention.

## Why the linear source is exactly invisible

Translation exchanges A and B while preserving q and E on every supported
torus. For either expectation F,

\[
F(p_A,p_B)=F(p_B,p_A),\qquad
\left.\partial_s F\right|_{s=0}=\frac{1-p}{2}\partial_p F.
\]

Thus the first variation is one common thermal reparameterization for both
geometries and both fields. Refind the pooled root and take Y_p/Q_p: its
Jacobian cancels and U_N'(0)=0. In particular p_0'(0)=−(1−p_0)/2.
The first-order null is exact even if the finite deformation changes the
irrelevant lattice coupling strongly.

The null hypothesis of a **common thermal clock for the whole family**,
F_g(p,s)=F_g(p_eff(p,s),0) for both q,E and both orientations, is much
stronger. It predicts U_N(s)=U_N(0) at every s, hence requires
2^(13/8)U_(N/2)(0)−U_N(0)=0. The endpoint data below are incompatible with
that requirement at their inherited precision. No alternative curve or
mixture is fit to manufacture this conclusion.

## A finite sum rule, not a guessed intermediate curve

On these finite tori Q increases with p, with nonzero derivative at the
root, including the matching endpoint. Its polynomial dependence and the
implicit-function theorem give a smooth root curve and U(s). Consequently

\[
U(1)-U(0)=\int_0^1(1-s)U''(s)\,ds.
\]

The positive endpoint difference forces positive curvature **somewhere**
along this family. It neither fixes U''(0), establishes monotonicity, nor
justifies a quadratic interpolation. A measurement only at s=0 can miss
the effect. Conversely rerunning s=1 would only check an exact mapping and
would not supply a new mechanism discriminator.

The next genuinely new experiment would have to distinguish explicit
predictions for the interior transmission, using this fixed microscopic
family and the same global U. No interior production was run and no
intermediate curve was fitted here; all prior lag1/contact stop decisions stand.
The endpoint scale factor also reflects the known change of microscopic
spacing, not a unique thermal-Q4/Jordan/operator fingerprint.
