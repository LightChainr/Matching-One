# Digital Alexander duality for the square 4/8 matching pair

Status: Phase-B proof bridge for Issue #269.

This note proves the strong rank-sum identity on an **honest periodic square
cellulation**.  Here honest means a finite regular square CW decomposition of
the torus: every vertex, edge and square has its usual embedded characteristic
map, and intersections of closed cells are common faces.  Quotients so small
that a square identifies its own vertices or edges are excluded from this
topological statement; the merged Phase-A exact oracle covers those declared
degenerate quotients separately.

All homology below has coefficients in `Q`.  Consequently the dimension of an
ambient image is the ordinary integer winding rank used by the code.

## The theorem

Let `T` be an honest periodic square cellulation of the oriented torus.  Color
every vertex black or white.  Let

- `B` be the embedded graph of black vertices and black nearest-neighbour
  edges;
- `W` be the white matching graph, with all white NN and plaquette-diagonal
  NNN edges;
- `r_b` and `r_w` be the ranks of the images of `H1(B;Q)` and `H1(W;Q)` in
  `H1(T;Q)`.

Then

```text
r_b+r_w=2.                                             (1)
```

In particular only `(r_b,r_w)=(0,2),(1,1),(2,0)` occur.  Therefore the
configuration matching observable, whether written with the `either` or
`cross` rank channel, is

```text
q = r_b-1 = 1-r_w = (r_b-r_w)/2.                       (2)
```

No percolation probability, CFT assumption, or asymptotic limit enters the
theorem.

## 1. Black NN graph and the black cubical complex have the same ambient image

Let `K_B` be the full cubical subcomplex spanned by the black vertices:

```text
black vertex                       -> 0-cell,
NN edge with black endpoints       -> 1-cell,
square with four black corners     -> 2-cell.
```

Its one-skeleton is exactly `B`.  Passing from `B` to `K_B` adds only black
square 2-cells.  On cellular chains,

```text
H1(B) -> H1(K_B)
```

is surjective and its kernel is generated by boundaries of those square
2-cells.  Every such boundary already bounds that same square in `T`, hence is
zero in `H1(T)`.  It follows that

```text
im[H1(B)->H1(T)] = im[H1(K_B)->H1(T)].                 (3)
```

This is deliberately an ambient-image statement.  The graph and cubical
complex can have different local cycle ranks.

## 2. The complementary dual is covered by white dual pixels

For every primal cell `sigma`, let `sigma*` be its complementary-dimensional
dual cell.  Define the complementary dual in the usual cubical sense,

```text
K_B^bullet = union{sigma*: sigma is not a cell of K_B}.
```

For a white primal vertex `v`, denote its closed dual square by `D_v`.  Then

```text
K_B^bullet = union_{v white} D_v.                      (4)
```

Indeed, a dual 2-cell is present exactly at a white vertex.  A dual edge is
present exactly when its primal edge has at least one white endpoint, so it is
a face of a white `D_v`.  A dual vertex is present exactly when its primal
plaquette is not all black, so it is a corner of at least one white `D_v`.
This proves both inclusions in (4).

The finite cover `{D_v}` is a good cubical cover after an arbitrarily small
open thickening.  Every nonempty finite intersection is a dual square, dual
edge, or dual vertex and is therefore contractible.  Its nerve `L_W` has:

- one vertex for every white site;
- an edge precisely for two white sites which are NN or are diagonal corners
  of one plaquette;
- a filled simplex for every three or four white corners of one plaquette.

Thus the one-skeleton of `L_W` is exactly the white matching graph `W`.
The cubical nerve lemma gives a homotopy equivalence

```text
|L_W| ~= K_B^bullet.                                   (5)
```

It may be chosen subordinate to the dual-cell cover.  Hence, after inclusion
in `T`, it is homotopic to the geometric realization which draws every NN or
diagonal edge inside its witnessing primal plaquette.

Adding the higher simplices of `L_W` to its one-skeleton only fills triangle
cycles supported in a single plaquette.  Cellular approximation makes
`H1(W)->H1(L_W)` surjective, and every element of its kernel maps to zero in
`H1(T)` because it is a sum of these plaquette-supported boundaries.  Combining
this with (5) gives the second image lemma:

```text
im[H1(W)->H1(T)]
 = im[H1(L_W)->H1(T)]
 = im[H1(K_B^bullet)->H1(T)].                          (6)
```

This is the exact reason that the bare king graph is allowed to contain local
triangles and crossed diagonals: those change graph Betti numbers, not ambient
torus winding rank.

There is also a crossing-free carrier which makes the local dictionary
explicit.  Let `H_W` have the white sites and one center `c_f` for every
plaquette with a white corner as vertices, with an edge `w--c_f` for every
white corner `w` of `f`.  Replace a matching edge `w--w'` by
`w--c_f--w'` in a witnessing plaquette.  Conversely, shortcut every passage
`w--c_f--w'` in a cycle of `H_W` to the matching edge `w--w'`.  The two paths
are homotopic inside `f`, so

```text
im[H1(W)->H1(T)] = im[H1(H_W)->H1(T)].                 (6a)
```

`H_W` is the one-dimensional carrier of the dual-pixel nerve; the higher
local cells of the nerve fill its plaquette-supported cycles.  Calling the
bare incidence graph a deformation retract would be too strong (for example,
in the all-white configuration it retains many local face cycles).  Equality
of the ambient image is exactly the invariant required here.

## 3. The 16 plaquette certificates

Write the white-corner mask in cyclic order `(SW,SE,NE,NW)`.  The sixteen
patterns reduce to six local certificates:

| white mask class | count | incidence carrier / nerve | pruned local spine |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `0000` | 1 | empty | empty |
| one white corner | 4 | one spoke to `c_f` / point | point |
| two adjacent whites | 4 | two-spoke path / interval | the white NN edge |
| two diagonal whites | 2 | two-spoke path / interval | the unique white diagonal |
| three white corners | 4 | three-spoke star / filled triangle | two-edge NN path; diagonal is redundant |
| `1111` | 1 | four-spoke star / filled 3-simplex | boundary square plus its local 2-cell; both diagonals are redundant |

The only diagonal that must remain in a graph spine is the two-white diagonal
case.  With three whites, the diagonal endpoints are joined by the two NN
edges through the third white corner.  With four whites, both diagonals are
replaced by boundary NN paths, while the local square 2-cell kills the
plaquette boundary.  These replacements occur inside one contractible
plaquette and preserve the ambient `H1` image.  The executable certificate
`scripts/digital_alexander_local_certificate.py` lists all sixteen masks and
checks these path replacements.

## 4. Complementary regular-neighbourhood sides

Choose a closed regular neighbourhood `N` of `K_B` in `T`, and put

```text
C = closure(T - int(N)).
```

The inclusion `K_B -> N` is a deformation equivalence.  Duncan--Schweinhart's
complementary-dual collapse (their Proposition 13) identifies
`K_B^bullet` with the topological complement of `K_B`; moving across the
collar of `partial N` identifies its ambient `H1` image with that of `C`.
Together with (3) and (6),

```text
V_b := im H1(B) = im H1(N),
V_w := im H1(W) = im H1(C).                            (7)
```

So the black NN and white matching ranks are precisely the ambient ranks of
the two complementary subsurfaces.

## 5. Poincare--Lefschetz proof of the rank sum

The long exact sequence of `(T,C)` gives

```text
im[H1(C)->H1(T)]
 = ker[H1(T)->H1(T,C)].                                (8)
```

Excision and Poincare--Lefschetz duality give

```text
H1(T,C) ~= H1(N,partial N) ~= H^1(N).                  (9)
```

Under (9), the map from `H1(T)` sends an ambient class `x` to the functional

```text
y |-> intersection_T(x, i_* y),  y in H1(N).           (10)
```

The intersection pairing on the oriented torus is nondegenerate.  Equations
(8)--(10) therefore say exactly

```text
V_w = V_b^perp.                                        (11)
```

Since `dim H1(T;Q)=2`, nondegeneracy gives

```text
dim V_b + dim V_w = 2,
```

which is (1).

This is also the `d=2,k=1` specialization of Duncan--Schweinhart, Theorem 14,
Equation (5): for a cubical complex and its complementary dual, the two
ambient homology-image ranks sum to the ambient torus Betti number.  Their
published proof uses the same complement collapse, relative long exact
sequence, and duality mechanism used above.

Reference: Paul Duncan and Benjamin Schweinhart, *Topological Phases in the
Plaquette Random-Cluster Model and Potts Lattice Gauge Theory*, Communications
in Mathematical Physics 406 (2025), Article 145; arXiv:2207.08339, Proposition
13 and Theorem 14.

## 6. From rank sum to the matching sign

Let

```text
q_either = 1[r_b>0]-1[r_w>0],
q_cross  = 1[r_b=2]-1[r_w=2].
```

By (1), the three possible rank pairs give respectively

```text
(0,2): q_either=q_cross=-1,
(1,1): q_either=q_cross= 0,
(2,0): q_either=q_cross=+1.
```

Thus both channel definitions coincide configurationwise and satisfy (2).
Taking expectations yields the exact finite-volume homological balance form

```text
M_N(p)=E[q]=1/2 (E[r_b]-E[r_w]).
```

## Scope boundary

The theorem covers regular periodic square cellulations and the standard
4-connected black / 8-connected white matching pair.  It does not assert that
arbitrary nonregular quotient graphs are regular CW complexes, nor does it
identify a continuum field.  Axis `L=2`, other short-period degeneracies, and
the declared C4 control are already covered configurationwise by the merged
Phase-A exact oracle; subdivision can provide a separate route to extend the
topological model, but is not needed for the theorem stated here.
