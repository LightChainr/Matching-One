# N=13 resolves projective birth and exit by two exact line orbits

The Gaussian quotient generated by `3+2i` has 13 sites. Its full Boolean
subset lattice has only 8,192 states and 53,248 directed site-addition edges,
so the birth/exit current can be computed exactly without enumerating the
`13!` random orderings.

The rank-one states split into two primitive-line orbits:

```text
axis:     ell in {(1,0),(0,1)},
diagonal: ell in {(1,1),(1,-1)}.
```

Their physical fourth characters are exact opposites,

```text
chi4_axis     = (-119 + 120 i)/169,
chi4_diagonal = ( 119 - 120 i)/169.
```

Both orbits occur at the first homology birth and at the second-rank exit.
For each orbit and every lower subset size `k`, the exact boundary census
satisfies

```text
J_birth(k)-J_exit(k)
  = (k+1) A_(k+1) - (N-k) A_k,
```

coefficientwise in the Bernstein representation of `A4(p)`. This is the
finite-volume source/sink identity `dA4/dp=j4_birth1-j4_exit2`, now resolved by
more than one inequivalent line orbit.

At the declared coordinate `p_ref=0.59274605079`, the two orbit contributions
are collinear and reinforce. The axis orbit contributes about 75.6% of the
total derivative. The diagonal orbit contributes the remaining 24.4% because
its birth-minus-exit incidence has the opposite sign while its `chi4` is also
opposite.

This rules out the simplest localization story in which the observed H4
derivative is only the dominant first-born line orbit and every other orbit
averages away. A minority orbit contributes coherently through a coupled
change of character sign and lifetime/source-sink imbalance.

The next exact comparison should vary the quotient while retaining the same
two-orbit basis. If the reinforcing shares organize by a modulus/cover
character, the mechanism is projective transport; if they vary independently,
the birth and exit operators need separate orbit couplings.

This is an exact finite-volume localization result, not a continuum-field or
asymptotic-amplitude identification.
