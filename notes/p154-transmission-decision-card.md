# A one-coefficient transmission map, rather than another lag sweep

Under the explicit two-rigid-birth-shift hypothesis
`e_g=-tau1 F1'_g`, `x_g=-tau2 F2'_g`, the **common shift cancels exactly
from root-comoving original U**. Only `delta=tau2-tau1` enters:

`v_clock=delta*K_rel`, where delta is calibrated from the pooled rank-one
population response and K_rel uses the baseline angular p-jets. No angular
gain, offset or exponent is fitted. This is a mechanism hypothesis with
an exact consequence, not a claim that the data obey rigid shifts.

The lag-one source gives nominal v_clock about **+.0098 atN260 and+.0175
atN340**, while the existing one-million-sample SE is about.722 and1.182.
At unchanged estimator efficiency, nominal3SE separation from angular
screening(v=0) would require about **49/41 billion** permutations.
This optimistic planning extrapolation even ignores coefficient uncertainty.
The longer ceil(sqrtN) lag is a different source and is kept separate.

Thus we do not launch a large fresh U block for these almost coincident
predictions, nor manufacture opposite signs from unresolved entry/completion
estimates. The useful next map must predict angular transmission beyond
this nearly screened rigid-clock mechanism, with measurable separation.
Large pooled entry/exit responses alone do not supply that map.
